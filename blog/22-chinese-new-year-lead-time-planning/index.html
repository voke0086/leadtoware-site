Chinese New Year 2027: How to Plan Your Sanitaryware Order Around the Shutdown

By the LEADTO team · Fengtang, Chaozhou, China

Chinese New Year 2027 falls on Saturday, 6 February. Seen from an office in Europe or the Americas, that looks like a one-week holiday. Seen from inside a ceramics factory, it is a logistics event that reshapes the whole first quarter: orders pile forward before it, everything stops during it, and production ramps back up slowly after it. Buyers who plan around the calendar get their containers out in January. Buyers who don't discover in February that their order is waiting behind a queue of everybody else's orders.

Why does a February holiday affect an order placed in November?

Three reasons, and none of them is the holiday week itself.

First, production slots. Orders placed in December compete for the same kiln capacity as every order other importers want finished before the shutdown, so realistic lead times stretch exactly when you want them shortest.

Second, labour. Many ceramic workers travel far home for the holiday, and part of the workforce changes factories during it. The first weeks back always run below full speed, so an official restart date is a start of the ramp-up, not the moment of full output.

Third, logistics. Trucking and vessel space tighten before the holiday and stay thin after it, because drivers and freight forwarders also go home. A finished, inspected container can still miss the last convenient sailing.

What actually happens around the shutdown?

A rough timeline — exact dates shift every year with the lunar calendar:

PeriodWhat happens at the factoryWhat it means for your order
Mid-Nov to mid-DecNormal production; workshops plan which orders finish before the shutdownBest window to place an order and lock a production slot
Late Dec to late JanPeak rush: forward-piled orders, raw material stocking, tighter vessel spaceLead times stretch; book freight early
Last two weeks before CNYFinal kiln loads and final loadings out; key QC staff and line supervisors leave firstInspection and loading supervision must be booked early
CNY weeksCasting, glazing, firing, trucking and port staffing all stopNo production, no loading, no inland transport
Two to three weeks afterWorkers return in waves; kilns reheat; output ramps graduallyTreat the official restart date as a beginning, not a finish

When should the order be placed to ship before the holiday?

Work backwards from the last shipping week of January. Production takes 15 to 25 days from deposit for standard models. Add pre-shipment inspection, container loading and trucking to port, plus the vessel booking lead time. That puts the safe ordering window in November and early December — which, for Chinese New Year 2027, means now.

What if the order cannot be finished before the shutdown?

Then the honest answer is a post-CNY production slot agreed in writing today, not a vague promise that everything will be fine. Ask the factory to commit to a production start date after the restart, confirm the first loading week, and build your own sales calendar on those dates. A realistic March delivery beats an optimistic February promise that slips twice. If you need goods urgently, discuss splitting the order: part finished and shipped in January, the rest in the first post-holiday production slots.

How should buyers communicate with the factory in the run-up?

Five questions, all worth answers in writing:

1. "Which production window can you confirm for my order, and from which date?" 2. "Will the full quantity be finished before your shutdown, or only part of it?" 3. "Can pre-shipment inspection and container loading supervision be completed before your key staff leave?" — this matters more than any other question, because inspection capacity also disappears during the holiday. 4. "What is your actual last loading day before the holiday?" 5. "When do you realistically restart, and when can the next order run at full speed?"

A factory that answers these precisely is telling you it has planned the season. Vague answers are the schedule risk showing up early.

Do prices change after Chinese New Year?

Labor and raw material costs in China often move after the holiday, and ocean freight follows its own seasonal cycle. Whatever a supplier's position is, ask one thing: the validity period of the quotation. A quotation with a stated validity protects both sides far better than a verbal assurance that "the price is stable".

Which commercial terms should stay the same during the rush?

Rush season is exactly when shortcuts get proposed, in both directions. Ours do not change:

- Samples of catalogue models carry a sample fee, refunded when you place the order. - Payment is 30% deposit with the order and the balance before the goods leave the factory, paired with open inspection and container loading supervision: you or your inspector verify the finished goods and watch the container sealed. We do not ask for payment against a copy of the bill of lading — before a holiday or after one. - Certification statements stay plain: our ceramic fixtures are CUPC & Watermark certified.

The short version

CNY 2027 lands on 6 February, but the production and freight calendar effectively closes in late January. Place orders now, get the production window and the last loading day in writing, book inspection before key staff leave, and treat the post-holiday restart realistically. The buyers who message factories in November are the ones whose containers sail in January.
