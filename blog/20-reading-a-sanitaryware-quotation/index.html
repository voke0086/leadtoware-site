How to Read a Sanitaryware Quotation: Where the Real Cost Hides

By the LEADTO team · Fengtang, Chaozhou, China

Two suppliers quote the same one-piece toilet. One says 46, the other 52. You take the cheaper one, and four months later the container lands with a lighter carton than expected, no seat covers, and a port bill you did not budget for. The unit price was never the price. A quotation is only useful if it is specific enough to be compared, disputed and enforced.

What must appear as a line, not as a sentence in an email?

A usable quotation is a document, not a number. Every item below should be visible on the page. If it only exists in the chat history, it does not exist.

Why can two factories quote the same model so differently?

Because "the same model" is rarely the same object, and the differences that move a price are rarely visible in a photograph:

- Wall thickness and weight. A heavier piece uses more clay, dries longer, fires longer — and survives freight better. - Glaze. Thickness, coverage on hidden surfaces, and whether the batch is controlled. Thin glaze on the trapway is where pinholes appear two years later. - Fittings. A named valve brand versus an unbranded one is a real cost difference, and the unbranded one is where service calls come from. - Sorting grade. A line that ships everything it fires versus one that pulls seconds has a different real yield, and that difference sits inside the price. - Packaging. Double-wall carton, moulded foam, pallet and corner protection are all paid for by someone.

The cheap quote is not necessarily dishonest. It is answering a different question. Your job is to make both suppliers answer the same one.

What is normally excluded from a sanitaryware quotation?

Read the exclusions before the price. The surprises cluster in the same few places: ocean freight and insurance unless the term is CIF; destination port charges, duties and clearance; inland delivery and unloading; installation and local plumbing labour; sample courier cost; tooling and mould maintenance for OEM work; and printing plates if you want your own carton artwork. Ask for these as a written list, so nothing arrives as a surprise invoice after the deposit is paid.

What should the payment clause actually say?

A payment clause is not a formality — it is the only leverage you keep after the deposit leaves your account. Ours is deliberately simple: 30% deposit with the order, balance paid before the goods leave the factory. That sequence is not an accident. It is paired with open inspection and container loading supervision: you or your nominated inspector check the finished, packed goods against the approved sample, and watch the container being loaded before the balance is released.

We do not ask for payment against a copy of the bill of lading, and you should be wary of suppliers who do. A container on the water cannot be inspected, sorted or stopped. Once the goods are loaded and the BL is issued, your remaining options are negotiation and lawyers. Keeping the balance until loading is inspected costs you nothing and protects both sides from the argument nobody wins.

How do samples and tooling show up in the price?

Samples of catalogue models carry a sample fee, and that fee is refunded when you place the order. It is charged because a sample is labour and kiln space, not a free brochure.

For OEM and ODM projects the numbers are larger and should never be a verbal agreement:

- Tooling is paid by the buyer, in the range of ten to several tens of thousands of RMB depending on the product and the number of moulds involved. - Tooling carries a minimum order quantity, agreed in writing before the mould is cut. - The tooling fee is credited back against accumulated shipments, so a steady programme recovers it. - The mould remains factory property, whether or not the credit has been fully earned. - Moulded products are available to other customers by default. A long-term partner can agree exclusivity in the contract; it is a commitment we will sign, not something assumed.

Put all five points in the quotation or the PO. A tooling payment without them is a payment with no defined end state.

How do you make a quotation enforceable?

1. Counter-sign it. A quotation signed by both sides, with a date, is the baseline for every later argument. 2. Attach the specification sheet. Dimensions, weight band, fitting brands, glaze reference, grade definition and inspection condition, on one page. 3. Name the reference sample. Model, glaze code and approval date, sealed and held by both parties. 4. Fix the change-order rule. Any change to model, packaging or quantity is re-quoted in writing before production, never absorbed silently. 5. Set the validity. Sixty days is normal; open-ended invites a price revision at the worst moment.

The short version

A quote you cannot compare is a quote you cannot control. Insist on the nine line items, read the exclusions, put the payment sequence and the inspection rights in the same paragraph, and write the sample and tooling terms down before money moves. The cheapest quotation is often the one that costs the most by the time the container is unpacked.
